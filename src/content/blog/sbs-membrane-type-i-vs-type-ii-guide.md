## Quick Answer: What Is the Difference Between Type I and Type II SBS Membrane?

**Type I and Type II are the two performance grades of SBS membrane in the GB 18242 / GB/T 18242 standard series — Type II is the higher grade.** Type II withstands 105°C heat resistance (Type I: 90°C), stays flexible to -25°C (Type I: -20°C), and requires higher tensile strength (≥800 N/50mm for polyester, versus ≥500) and elongation (≥40%, versus ≥30%). Grade is independent of thickness: a 4mm Type I and a 4mm Type II are different products with different ratings and prices. Specify Type II for cold climates, hot dark roofs, high-load structures and projects with extended warranty requirements.

## What Are Type I and Type II Grades?

The grade is a material performance classification set by the standard, not a brand tier and not a thickness. In the product marking, the grade appears right after the membrane type — for example **SBS II PY 4mm PE** means: SBS membrane, **Type II** grade, polyester (PY) reinforcement, 4mm nominal thickness, PE film surface. Type I and Type II differ on three dimensions:

1. **Heat resistance** — the maximum temperature at which the membrane does not flow or drip (90°C for Type I, 105°C for Type II).
2. **Low-temperature flexibility** — the lowest temperature at which the membrane bends without cracking (-20°C for Type I, -25°C for Type II).
3. **Mechanical requirements** — tensile strength and elongation, which are higher for Type II.

## Type I vs Type II: Performance Compared

> The values below are from the former GB 18242-2008 requirements. The current standard is GB/T 18242-2025 (effective 2026-05-01); confirm current limits with the latest standard and the manufacturer's TDS.

| Property | Type I | Type II |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Heat resistance | 90°C — no flow or drip | 105°C — no flow or drip |
| Low-temperature flexibility | -20°C — no crack | -25°C — no crack |
| Peak tensile — polyester (PY) | ≥500 N/50mm | ≥800 N/50mm |
| Elongation at peak — polyester (PY) | ≥30% | ≥40% |
| Peak tensile — glass fiber (G) | ≥350 N/50mm | ≥500 N/50mm |
| Watertightness (30 min) | 0.3MPa (G: 0.2MPa) | 0.3MPa |
| Low-temperature flexibility after heat aging | -15°C | -20°C |

Two numbers worth noticing:

- **The 5°C low-temperature margin.** Type II stays crack-free at -25°C versus -20°C for Type I. On a roof that sees -22°C in winter, that margin is the difference between a working membrane and a cracked one.
- **Heat aging shifts the rating.** After 10 days of heat aging, the low-temperature rating relaxes to -15°C (Type I) and -20°C (Type II). A membrane that barely passes its fresh-state rating may fail after aging — another reason the extra margin of Type II matters on demanding projects.

## When Should You Specify Type II?

Type II is not always required — but it becomes effectively mandatory in four situations:

| Trigger | Why Type II | Reference value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Winter temperature approaches or falls below -20°C | Type II keeps a 5°C margin at -25°C | Low-temperature flexibility |
| Roof surface temperature approaches 90°C (dark exposed roofs in hot regions) | Type II resists 105°C without flow or drip | Heat resistance |
| High loads or structural settlement expected | Type II requires ≥800 N/50mm (PY), nearly double Type I's requirement | Tensile strength |
| Project specification or warranty demands the higher grade | Many public and institutional specs default to Type II | Contract documents |

For moderate climates with stable substrates and no special warranty terms, Type I can be adequate and more economical. When in doubt, confirm the required grade in the project specification before ordering — the grade is part of the product code, so it cannot be upgraded after the membrane is manufactured.

## Grade vs Thickness vs Reinforcement: Three Independent Variables

Grade, thickness and reinforcement are separate specification variables, and each controls different properties:

- **Grade (Type I / II)** controls temperature performance: heat resistance, low-temperature flexibility and the associated tensile/elongation minimums.
- **Thickness (3mm / 4mm / 5mm)** controls material content — soluble content rises from about 2,100 g/m² at 3mm to 2,900 at 4mm and 3,500 at 5mm — and with it puncture resistance and long-term durability.
- **Reinforcement (PY / G / PYG)** controls how the membrane responds to movement and load: polyester stretches, glass fiber stays dimensionally stable.

A common specification error is treating them as one decision. See our [3mm vs 4mm vs 5mm SBS Membrane guide](/blog/3mm-vs-4mm-sbs-waterproofing-membrane/) for the thickness logic and [PY vs G vs PYG Reinforcement guide](/blog/py-g-pyg-sbs-membrane-reinforcement-guide/) for the carrier logic.

## Three Common Misconceptions

1. **"Type II is just a thicker Type I."** No — grade and thickness are independent. Type II is a higher performance grade with higher temperature ratings and tensile requirements, available in all thicknesses. A 4mm Type I and a 4mm Type II are different products.
2. **"Type I means lower quality."** Type I is a compliant, standard-grade product for moderate conditions — not a reject grade. It is simply specified for projects where the higher Type II ratings are not required.
3. **"The low-temperature rating means I can install it at that temperature."** No — the -20°C / -25°C rating describes in-service performance after installation. Application temperature follows separate TDS rules: substrate preparation, priming and working conditions still apply. ## What to Specify When Buying

A complete SBS specification names the grade explicitly: **SBS + grade + reinforcement + thickness + surface + standard** — for example "SBS II PY 4mm PE, GB/T 18242". A quotation that only says "4mm SBS" is incomplete, and the grade is the single most common omission.

When you compare suppliers, request the TDS and batch test report with every quotation and verify that the tested heat resistance, low-temperature flexibility and tensile values match the grade specified. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between Type I and Type II SBS membrane?

Type I and Type II are the two performance grades in the GB 18242 / GB/T 18242 standard series. Type II is the higher grade: it withstands 105°C heat resistance versus 90°C for Type I, remains flexible to -25°C versus -20°C, and requires higher tensile strength (at least 800 N/50mm for polyester reinforcement versus 500 for Type I) and elongation (at least 40% versus 30%). Grade is independent of thickness — a 4mm Type I and a 4mm Type II are different products. Type II is specified for cold climates, hot dark roofs, high-load structures and projects with extended warranty requirements.

### When should I specify Type II SBS membrane?

Specify Type II when any of these apply: winter temperatures approach or fall below -20°C (Type II adds a 5°C margin at -25°C); roof surface temperature can approach 90°C, typical of dark exposed roofs in hot regions (Type II resists 105°C); the structure is subject to high loads or settlement (Type II requires at least 800 N/50mm tensile for polyester reinforcement); or the project specification or warranty requires the higher grade. For moderate climates with stable conditions and no movement concerns, Type I may be adequate. Confirm the grade with the project specification and the membrane TDS.

### Is Type II SBS membrane thicker than Type I?

No — grade and thickness are independent specification variables. Type II is a performance grade with higher heat resistance (105°C), low-temperature flexibility (-25°C) and tensile requirements; thickness (3mm, 4mm or 5mm) controls material content, puncture resistance and system design. Both grades are available in all standard thicknesses. A 4mm Type I and a 4mm Type II are the same thickness but different products with different ratings and prices. Specify both values separately — for example SBS II PY 4mm PE.

### Does Type II SBS membrane cost more?

Yes — Type II commands a premium because the formulation must meet higher heat and cold performance plus higher tensile and elongation requirements, which means more modifier and tighter production control. The premium is justified where the project demands it: cold climates, hot dark roofs, high-load structures or warranty requirements. To compare prices fairly, compare identical specifications — same grade, thickness, reinforcement and surface finish — and request the TDS and batch test report to verify the grade actually supplied.

### Which SBS membrane grade should I choose for cold climates?

Choose Type II for cold climates. Its low-temperature flexibility rating of -25°C (versus -20°C for Type I) provides the margin needed where winter temperatures drop well below freezing, including high-altitude sites and refrigerated facilities. Remember that the low-temperature rating describes in-service performance — application temperature is a separate consideration, and installation conditions follow the TDS. Pair Type II with polyester (PY) reinforcement where the structure can also settle or move.
