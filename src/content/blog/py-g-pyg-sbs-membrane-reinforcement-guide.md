## Quick Answer: Which SBS Reinforcement Should You Choose?

**Choose the reinforcement by substrate movement, not by price.** Polyester (PY) stretches with moving and settling substrates — Type II requires at least 40% elongation at peak. Glass fiber (G) is rigid with near-zero elongation, best as a base layer in multi-layer roof systems on stable decks. Composite (PYG) combines both carriers into one sheet for the highest tensile strength — at least 900 N/50mm Type II — and is specified for mechanically fixed single-layer roofing. All three comply with GB/T 18242-2008, but they are different products: a 4mm PY and a 4mm G are not interchangeable at the same price.

## What Are PY, G and PYG Reinforcement?

The reinforcement — also called the carrier or felt — is the internal layer that gives the membrane its mechanical strength. The SBS-modified bitumen provides waterproofing and flexibility, but it is the carrier that decides how the sheet responds to tensile load, temperature change and puncture. GB/T 18242-2008 recognizes three carriers, and each has a different internal structure:

1. **PY — polyester felt.** A non-woven mat of randomly laid polyester fibres. The fibres interlock in all directions, so the mat is elastic: it stretches, recovers, and absorbs movement without tearing.
2. **G — glass fiber mat.** A woven grid of glass strands. The grid is rigid and dimensionally stable, but it does not stretch — elongation is effectively zero.
3. **PYG — glass-reinforced polyester (composite).** A polyester felt bonded with an embedded glass fiber grid. One sheet carries both the elasticity of the felt and the stability of the grid.

## Reinforcement Performance Compared (Type II)

The numbers below are the Type II requirements in GB/T 18242-2008, with the factory test results for our TY-SHIELD™ PY membrane shown in parentheses:

| Property (Type II) | PY (Polyester) | G (Glass Fiber) | PYG (Composite) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Peak tensile — max peak (N/50mm) | ≥800 (900+ tested) | ≥500 | ≥900 (second peak ≥800) |
| Elongation at max peak | ≥40% | No requirement (near zero) | — |
| Elongation at second peak | — | — | ≥15% |
| Dimensional change after heat aging | ≤0.7% | — | ≤0.3% |
| Nail shank tear strength (N) | — | — | ≥300 |
| Standard thicknesses | 3 / 4 / 5mm | 3 / 4mm | 5mm |
| Typical role | Primary waterproofing on moving structures | Base layer of multi-layer systems | Mechanically fixed single layer |

Three details in this table are easy to miss and worth knowing:

- **Glass fiber has no elongation requirement.** The standard does not even test it — a G membrane is expected to stay put, not stretch. If the substrate can move, G is the wrong carrier.
- **PYG is tested at two peaks.** During a tensile test the glass grid breaks first (second peak), then the polyester felt carries on. The second-peak requirement of ≥800 N/50mm is what keeps the sheet intact after the grid fails.
- **Thickness is restricted by carrier.** Under the standard, G is only available in 3mm and 4mm, and PYG only in 5mm. You cannot order a 5mm G or a 3mm PYG from a compliant factory — thickness and carrier are linked.

## How to Choose: Match the Reinforcement to the Job

**Tunnels, foundations and below-grade structures → PY.** Buried structures settle, shift and crack over time. The membrane must bridge those cracks without rupturing, and only PY offers the elongation to do it. Underground waterproofing standards in China have used polyester as the default carrier for this reason.

**Stable decks in multi-layer roof systems → G.** Where the deck is rigid — reinforced concrete with minimal movement — a G base layer provides dimensional stability and supports the cap sheet above it. It is also the most economical option for the base ply, which is never exposed.

**Large-span roofs installed by mechanical fixing → PYG.** Mechanically fixed single-layer systems hold the membrane with fasteners, so the carrier must resist nail tear — PYG is the only carrier with a nail shank tear requirement (≥300 N). The composite construction also keeps dimensional change below 0.3% so the sheet does not wrinkle between fixings.

When in doubt, PY is the safe default: it performs acceptably on stable decks and is the only carrier that survives movement. G and PYG are specialist choices for specific system designs.

## Three Common Misconceptions

1. **"G is just the cheap version of PY."** It is not a downgrade — it is a different design. G trades all elongation for dimensional stability. Choosing G on a moving substrate is a specification error, not a cost saving.
2. **"PYG is a double layer of membrane."** No — PYG is one membrane with one composite carrier. The glass grid and polyester felt are bonded inside a single sheet, not installed as two plies.
3. **"Thickness matters more than the carrier."** The carrier and the thickness are independent variables, and both must be specified. A 4mm G and a 4mm PY are completely different products, with different prices, test values and applications. ## What to Specify When Buying

A complete SBS specification names five things: **carrier (PY / G / PYG), performance grade (Type I / II), thickness (3 / 4 / 5mm), surface finish (PE / S / M / ALU) and standard (GB/T 18242-2008)**. A quotation that only says "4mm SBS" is incomplete — the carrier alone can move the price by a meaningful margin.

When you compare suppliers, ask for the carrier weight in grams per square meter (g/m²), not just the word "polyester". A heavier felt delivers better puncture resistance and tear strength, and the difference shows up in the batch test report. Request the TDS and the batch test report with every quotation, and check that the tested tensile and elongation values match the carrier you specified. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between PY, G and PYG reinforcement in SBS membrane?

PY (polyester felt) is an elastic non-woven carrier with the highest elongation — Type II requires at least 40% at peak — so the membrane stretches with moving substrates. G (glass fiber mat) is a rigid woven grid with near-zero elongation, used mainly as a base layer in multi-layer roof systems on stable decks. PYG (glass-reinforced polyester) bonds both carriers into one sheet, reaching the highest tensile strength (at least 900 N/50mm Type II) with dimensional change below 0.3%, and is specified for mechanically fixed single-layer roofing. The carrier is part of the product code — for example SBS II PY 4mm PE — so confirm it in every quotation.

### Which SBS reinforcement is best for tunnels and foundations?

For tunnels, foundations and other below-grade structures that settle or move, choose polyester (PY) reinforcement. Its Type II elongation of at least 40% lets the membrane bridge cracks and absorb differential settlement without rupturing, and PY is the standard carrier for underground waterproofing under GB/T 18242-2008. Glass fiber and composite carriers trade elongation for dimensional stability and are rarely the right choice on moving substrates. Review the expected settlement and substrate movement data with the project engineer before finalizing the specification.

### Is PYG reinforcement a double layer of membrane?

No — PYG (glass-reinforced polyester) is a single membrane containing one composite carrier: a polyester felt bonded with an embedded glass fiber grid. It is not two piles of membrane and it is not installed as a double-ply system. The composite gives one sheet both high tensile strength (at least 900 N/50mm Type II, with a second peak of at least 800) and low dimensional change (below 0.3%), which is why it is specified for mechanically fixed single-layer roofing. Under GB/T 18242-2008, PYG is listed at 5mm thickness.

### Does the reinforcement type affect the price of SBS membrane?

Yes — reinforcement is one of the main price drivers in an SBS quotation. Polyester and composite carriers cost more than glass fiber because the raw materials and processing are more expensive, and the same thickness in a different carrier is a different product at a different price. Always compare quotations on identical specifications: carrier (PY, G or PYG), thickness, performance grade and surface finish. Request the TDS and batch test report to verify the actual carrier weight per square meter before comparing unit prices.

### Can glass fiber (G) SBS membrane handle substrate movement?

Glass fiber reinforcement has near-zero elongation, so it cannot stretch with a moving or settling substrate — under differential movement it can crack rather than bridge the gap. G carriers belong on stable substrates, typically as the base layer of a multi-layer roof system where thermal movement is minimal. If the structure is subject to settlement, thermal cycling or seismic activity, specify polyester (PY) reinforcement instead, and confirm the choice against the project specification before ordering.
