## Quick Answer: How Much Fits, and What Actually Limits It

**A 20FT container loads approximately 400-500 rolls (4,000-5,000 m²) of 3mm SBS membrane, 300-400 rolls (3,000-4,000 m²) of 4mm, and 250-350 rolls (2,500-3,500 m²) of 5mm.** The constraint changes with thickness: 3mm and 4mm loadings are set by pallet and container geometry, while **5mm is limited by weight, not volume** — 350 rolls weigh about 19 t, close to the effective payload ceiling of many 20FT routings. For 40FT containers, roughly double the m² is available, but the payload cap makes the loading weight-limited again.

## Why Container Loading Math Belongs in Your Procurement Plan

Container loading is not a shipping detail — it is a pricing and budgeting input:

1. **Freight cost per m².** A 20FT container's ocean freight is roughly fixed regardless of whether it holds 4,500 m² of 3mm or 3,000 m² of 4mm. The thinner the specification, the lower the freight cost per square meter.
2. **MOQ alignment.** Most factories quote a minimum order of one 20FT container per specification. Knowing the loading range tells you whether your project volume fits one container or requires several.
3. **Budget accuracy.** Total landed cost = product + freight + local logistics. Container count drives the freight component, and the difference between 4 and 5 containers is significant on most routes.
4. **Mixed loading planning.** Different thicknesses, reinforcement types or surface finishes can share one container — but the mix changes both weight and volume, which is exactly where buyers get their calculations wrong.

## What Determines How Much Fits: The Five Variables

| Variable | Impact on Loading | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Roll dimensions | Sets m² per roll and roll footprint | Standard 1m × 10m = 10 m²/roll |
| Thickness | Drives roll diameter and weight | 3mm vs 5mm: ~65% more compound per m² |
| Reinforcement & surface | Changes roll weight (sand/granules add kg) | Mineral granule surfacing +2-4 kg/roll |
| Pallet configuration | Determines how many rolls per pallet and pallet footprint | Vertical rolls, HDPE-wrapped pallets |
| Weight limits | Can override volume entirely | Destination-port or carrier payload caps |

## The Calculation in Four Steps

**Step 1 — Roll area.** Standard rolls are 1m wide × 10m long = **10 m² per roll**. Custom roll sizes change this number, so confirm it first.

**Step 2 — Roll count.** Divide the project area by the roll area:

```
Rolls = Total area (m²) ÷ 10 m² per roll
```

**Step 3 — Weight check.** Multiply the roll count by the typical roll weight, then compare with the container payload:

```
Total weight (kg) = Rolls × weight per roll (kg)
```

**Step 4 — Compare with the container.** A 20GP has about 33 m³ internal volume (ISO 668) and a maximum payload that varies by carrier and destination — commonly in the 21-28 t range, with many routings limited lower. Always confirm the effective limit with your forwarder, not the theoretical maximum.

**Worked example — 3mm, 20FT:** 4,500 m² ÷ 10 = 450 rolls; 450 × 35 kg ≈ **15.8 t** — comfortably inside the payload, so geometry rules.

**Worked example — 5mm, 20FT:** 3,500 m² ÷ 10 = 350 rolls; 350 × 55 kg ≈ **19.3 t** — close to the effective limit, so weight rules.

## Why 5mm Is a Weight-Limited Product

The most common mistake in container planning is assuming volume decides everything. For thick specifications it does not:

- 3mm rolls weigh roughly **33-38 kg** each; 4mm roughly **43-48 kg**; 5mm roughly **53-58 kg** (PE-faced, standard rolls — granules add 2-4 kg).
- A full 5mm loading of 350 rolls reaches **~19 t**, leaving little margin against the 21-22 t effective limits applied on many Asia-Europe and Asia-US routings.
- In practice this means 5mm projects often need **more containers than the m² suggests** — plan freight cost per m² accordingly, or evaluate whether the specification genuinely requires 5mm (see our [thickness selection guide](/blog/3mm-vs-4mm-sbs-waterproofing-membrane/)).

## Pallet Configuration and Export Packing

Standard export packing for SBS membrane is **vertical rolls on fumigated wooden pallets**, wrapped in high-density polyethylene film with corner protectors and heavy-duty strapping. This configuration:

- Protects roll edges and prevents deformation in transit
- Allows forklift handling at origin and destination
- Keeps rolls accessible for inspection without full unpacking

The number of rolls per pallet (typically 24-36 depending on thickness) sets the pallet footprint, which in turn drives how many pallets fit across the container width — the practical reason final loading figures are quoted as ranges rather than single numbers.

## Common Mistakes in Container Planning

1. **Volume-only math.** Applying the container's cubic volume to rolls without accounting for pallet voids and roll geometry overestimates loading by 20-30%.
2. **Ignoring payload.** A 40FT container has about twice the volume of a 20FT but not twice the weight capacity — thick specifications hit the weight ceiling first.
3. **Using the theoretical payload.** The 28 t "maximum" on the container spec sheet is not the operating limit on many routes; the forwarder's confirmed limit is what matters.
4. **Assuming all rolls weigh the same.** Granule-surfaced and polyester-reinforced rolls weigh more than PE-faced fiberglass rolls — one mixed container can silently cross the weight limit.

## What to Send Your Supplier for an Accurate Loading Plan

To get a loading plan you can budget against, provide all five parameters together:

1. **Thickness** (3mm / 4mm / 5mm)
2. **Performance grade** (Type I / Type II)
3. **Reinforcement** (polyester PY / fiberglass G / composite PYG)
4. **Surface finish** (PE film / sand / mineral granules)
5. **Destination port and any container size preference** (20FT / 40FT)

The factory will confirm rolls per pallet, pallets per container, total m² and total weight — the four numbers you need for the freight quotation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many rolls of SBS membrane fit in a 20FT container?

With standard 1m × 10m rolls (10 m² each), a 20FT container loads approximately 400-500 rolls of 3mm (4,000-5,000 m²), 300-400 rolls of 4mm (3,000-4,000 m²), and 250-350 rolls of 5mm (2,500-3,500 m²). The exact count depends on roll weight, reinforcement type, surface finish, pallet configuration and destination-port weight limits, so confirm the loading plan with the factory before finalizing your order.

### How many m² of SBS membrane fit in a 40FT container?

A 40GP container offers roughly twice the internal volume of a 20GP but a similar maximum payload (about 26 t, carrier-dependent), so 40FT loading is weight-limited. As a reference estimate, 3mm loads approximately 6,500-8,000 m², 4mm approximately 5,500-6,500 m², and 5mm approximately 4,500-5,500 m². These figures assume standard 1m × 10m rolls; request the factory loading plan for your exact specification and destination.

### Why is 5mm SBS membrane limited by weight rather than volume?

5mm contains roughly 65% more bitumen compound per m² than 3mm, so each roll is much heavier: about 53-58 kg versus 33-38 kg for 3mm. 350 rolls of 5mm (3,500 m²) weigh approximately 19 t, already close to the effective 21-22 t payload limit of many 20FT routings — while occupying far less than the container's full volume. Weight, not volume, sets the ceiling for thick specifications.

### How much does one roll of SBS membrane weigh?

Typical gross weights for standard 1m × 10m rolls: 3mm PE-faced about 33-38 kg, 4mm PE-faced about 43-48 kg, and 5mm PE-faced about 53-58 kg. Mineral granule surfacing adds roughly 2-4 kg per roll, and polyester reinforcement adds a little more than fiberglass. Use the product TDS and the factory packing list for the exact figure for your configuration.

### How do I calculate how many containers my project needs?

Divide the total project area by the roll area (10 m² for standard 1m × 10m rolls) to get the roll count, multiply by the typical roll weight to get total weight, then compare with the container payload. Example: 20,000 m² of 3mm membrane = 2,000 rolls at about 35 kg each ≈ 70 t ≈ 4-5 × 20FT containers. For a reliable budget, ask the factory for a container loading plan based on your specification and destination port.
