## Quick Answer: Which Thickness Do You Actually Need?

**4mm is the most common specification for single-layer SBS membrane systems, but the right thickness depends on the system design — not on climate.** Low-temperature flexibility is set by the performance grade (Type I -20°C / Type II -25°C), not by thickness. Thickness controls puncture resistance, durability, system layers and container loading efficiency. Choose 3mm for two-layer base sheets, 4mm for single-layer exposed roofing, and 5mm only where the project specification demands it.

## The Most Common Mistake: Thickness vs. Performance Grade

Many B2B buyers assume a thicker membrane is automatically better in cold weather. It is not. The two specifications answer different questions:

| What Thickness Controls | What the Grade (Type I / II) Controls |
| :--- | :--- |
| Puncture resistance | Low-temperature flexibility (-20°C / -25°C) |
| Long-term durability | Heat resistance (90°C / 105°C) |
| Number of system layers | Tensile strength and elongation |
| Container loading (m² per 20FT) | — |

A 3mm Type II membrane and a 4mm Type II membrane have the **same** -25°C rating. If your project is in a cold climate, the Type II grade is the specification that matters — the thickness decision is about system design and budget.

## 3mm vs 4mm vs 5mm at a Glance

| Property | 3mm | 4mm | 5mm |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Soluble Content** | ≥ 2,100 g/m² | ≥ 2,900 g/m² | ≥ 3,500 g/m² |
| **Typical Role** | Base sheet in two-layer systems | Single-layer exposed roofing; cap sheet | Heavy-duty / spec-required projects |
| **Puncture Resistance** | Standard | Higher | Highest |
| **20FT Container Loading (ref.)** | ~4,000-5,000 m² | ~3,000-4,000 m² | ~2,500-3,500 m² |
| **Cost per m²** | Lowest | Standard | Highest |
| **Cold-Weather Rating** | Type I / II (grade-based) | Type I / II (grade-based) | Type I / II (grade-based) |

*Loading figures are references — final quantities depend on reinforcement, surface finish, pallet configuration and destination-port weight limits.*

## Thickness in System Context

Thickness only makes sense in the context of the waterproofing system:

- **Two-layer systems (the most common for roofing and underground work).** A 3mm base sheet is installed first, followed by a 4mm granulated cap sheet. The industry-standard hybrid approach documented by the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association (ARMA) follows this base-sheet + cap-sheet logic, and SBS is the preferred base-sheet chemistry because its elastomeric modification bonds well to the substrate.
- **Single-layer exposed systems.** 4mm is the standard minimum for exposed flat roofs — it provides adequate puncture resistance and service life without the cost of 5mm.
- **Heavy-duty and special specifications.** 5mm appears in tenders for high-traffic decks, heavy protection requirements, or regional specifications that call it out explicitly. Use it only when specified — the cost per m² and the reduced container loading rarely justify it otherwise.

## Selection Logic in Three Questions

| Question | If Yes | If No |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Is the membrane part of a two-layer system? | **3mm base sheet** + 4mm cap | Go to next question |
| Is it a single exposed layer? | **4mm** | Go to next question |
| Does the tender specify 5mm or heavy-duty loading? | **5mm** | Confirm minimum thickness with the project spec |

## Thickness and Your Procurement Cost

Thickness is one of the largest levers on landed cost:

1. **Loading efficiency.** 3mm loads ~25% more m² per container than 4mm — the freight cost per m² rises with thickness.
2. **Unit price.** The higher soluble content of 4mm and 5mm means more raw material per m², and a correspondingly higher unit price.
3. **System-level math.** A 3mm + 4mm two-layer system costs more than a 4mm single layer — compare total system cost, not per-roll price.

## What to Include When Requesting a Quotation

When asking for pricing, specify all four parameters together — missing one changes the product:

1. **Thickness** (3mm / 4mm / 5mm)
2. **Performance grade** (Type I / Type II)
3. **Reinforcement** (polyester PY / fiberglass G / composite PYG)
4. **Surface finish** (PE film / sand / mineral granules / aluminum foil)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What thickness of SBS membrane should I use for a flat roof?

For a single-layer exposed flat roof system, 4mm is the most common specification, offering the best balance of puncture resistance and installation reliability. For two-layer systems, a 3mm base sheet combined with a 4mm granulated cap sheet is the standard arrangement. 5mm is reserved for heavy-duty or high-traffic areas and project specifications that explicitly require it. Confirm the minimum thickness with the project specification before ordering.

### Can 3mm SBS membrane be used as a single layer?

Generally not recommended. 3mm SBS membrane is designed as a base sheet in two-layer systems, where a cap sheet protects it. Used as a single exposed layer, 3mm offers limited puncture resistance and durability compared with 4mm, and many project specifications require a minimum 4mm for single-ply exposed roofing. If a single layer is required, specify at least 4mm.

### What is the difference between 3mm and 4mm SBS membrane?

The core difference is material content and the properties that follow from it: 3mm has a soluble content of about 2,100 g/m² versus 2,900 g/m² for 4mm, giving 4mm higher puncture resistance and long-term durability. A 20FT container loads approximately 4,000-5,000 m² of 3mm membrane versus 3,000-4,000 m² of 4mm, so 3mm is cheaper per m² and per container. Low-temperature and heat performance are identical when the grade (Type I or II) is the same.

### Does a thicker SBS membrane perform better in cold weather?

No. Low-temperature flexibility is determined by the performance grade, not the thickness: Type I is rated at -20°C and Type II at -25°C regardless of whether the membrane is 3mm, 4mm or 5mm. Thickness affects puncture resistance, durability and system design, not cold-weather performance. For cold climates, choose Type II and confirm the grade on the TDS rather than assuming a thicker membrane is more cold-resistant.

### How does thickness affect SBS membrane price and container loading?

Thickness directly changes loading efficiency and unit cost. As a reference, a 20FT container holds approximately 4,000-5,000 m² of 3mm, 3,000-4,000 m² of 4mm, and 2,500-3,500 m² of 5mm membrane, so thicker specifications carry higher freight cost per m². 3mm is the economical choice for two-layer base sheets; 4mm is the single-layer standard; 5mm adds cost only where the specification demands it.
