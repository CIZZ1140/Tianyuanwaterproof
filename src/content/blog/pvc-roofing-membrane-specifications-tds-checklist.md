**To compare PVC roofing membrane specifications, match the exact product, thickness definition, reinforcement and backing, test methods, and proposed roof assembly. Then price the same net coverage and supply scope.** A matching thickness or a lower roll price alone does not establish an equivalent offer.

**A technical data sheet (TDS)** summarises the supplier's declared product properties and their measurement basis. It is one part of a technical submission, alongside the relevant reports, system documents and agreed order requirements. It is not automatically a certificate for every delivery batch.

This checklist is for importers, distributors and project procurement teams evaluating PVC roofing sheets before a bulk order. If the material family is still undecided, use our [PVC versus TPO roofing selection guide](/blog/pvc-vs-tpo-roofing-membrane-selection/). Here the focus is on reading the documents and resolving differences between PVC offers.

## Start by matching the document to the offered roll

Before comparing performance numbers, check the product name, code and document revision. Ask the supplier to identify the configuration being quoted: reinforced or unreinforced construction, backing, nominal thickness, surface colour and intended application. A family brochure can cover several products; its most favourable values may not describe the one on your quotation.

Tianyuan's [PVC waterproofing membrane product page](/products/pvc-waterproofing-membrane-manufacturer/) lists available options for a supply discussion. For an order, ask which current TDS, relevant test reports and installation instructions Tianyuan can supply for the proposed configuration. Request the document list with the quotation; confirm any unavailable evidence before technical acceptance.

A practical review separates the following document roles:

| Document | What to look for | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Technical data sheet | Product identity, construction, declared properties, methods and revision | That a particular delivery batch has been tested |
| Type-test report | Tested product, construction, method, conditions, date and results | That every delivered batch was tested or that a different construction is covered |
| Batch or inspection record, where agreed | Batch identity, checks performed, results and acceptance basis | That untested characteristics or the whole roof assembly have been verified |
| System documents and drawings | Compatible layers, attachment and details relevant to the roof | That a substitution is acceptable without review |

If the product code differs between documents, resolve the mismatch before treating the numbers as a single evidence package. A family-level report needs a clear basis for its applicability to the offered sheet. A batch record should identify the batch being supplied; do not assume that a type-test report is batch-specific.

## Define thickness before comparing prices

Ask what the quoted thickness actually describes. Is it a nominal sheet thickness or a minimum requirement? Does it include a fleece backing? Is a separate measurement over reinforcement specified? Record the measurement basis and applicable method alongside the value.

The visible bulk of a backed sheet is not a reliable measure of its waterproofing layer. An apparently thicker product may include material that the project's membrane-thickness requirement excludes. Conversely, matching one nominal number does not establish that two sheets have the same construction.

When quotations use different units, keep the original units and show any conversion separately. **One mil is one thousandth of an inch, equal to 0.0254 mm.** A mil is not a millimetre. Rounding a converted number does not demonstrate compliance with a minimum thickness or tolerance requirement.

For unit conversion, [NIST's length-unit explanation](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/si-units-length) gives the inch-to-metre relationship. The mil conversion above follows from that relationship and the definition of a mil.

### Example: why two thickness quotations may not be comparable

**Illustrative quotation wording, not Tianyuan product data or actual supplier offers:**

| Quotation | What is declared | Procurement decision |
|---|---|---|
| Offer A | Membrane thickness excluding backing; tolerance and method stated | Compare with the project's membrane-thickness requirement |
| Offer B | Total finished thickness including backing; membrane-only thickness not stated | Request the membrane-only definition and value before calling the offers equivalent |

Neither offer is automatically better. Offer B needs clarification; this wording alone does not prove that its membrane is thinner or non-compliant.

## Read reinforcement and backing as separate fields

Internal reinforcement and an underside backing describe different parts of the sheet. A fleece-backed option may also be reinforced. Do not place “reinforced” and “fleece-backed” into mutually exclusive categories without checking the actual construction.

Request a written description of the carrier and backing, together with the proposed attachment method. Product codes and type labels belong to their stated standard or manufacturer's naming system; do not assume that a letter on one document translates directly into a type number on another.

For example, tensile stress reported in MPa and force reported in N/50 mm are not directly interchangeable. Read the method, specimen construction and direction before comparing results. Do not use an improvised unit conversion to bridge tests that measure different things.

## Make a specification review table

Use the following fields to build a comparison schedule. Add separate columns for the project requirement and each supplier's documented offer. Mark unanswered fields as open, rather than copying values from a different product.

| Specification field | Record in the comparison | Clarify before accepting |
|---|---|---|
| Product and intended use | Code, revision, exposed roofing or another application | Is this the exact offered roofing sheet? |
| Thickness and dimensions | Definition, tolerance, width and length | Is backing included, and what is the net coverage? |
| Reinforcement and backing | Carrier and underside construction | Does it match the proposed attachment and details? |
| Mechanical properties | Method, units, direction and specimen identity | Are both offers being compared on the same basis? |
| Watertightness | Test method, conditions and result | Is the test applicable to this configuration? |
| Weathering or low-temperature properties | Conditions, duration where relevant and acceptance criterion | Is a laboratory result being mistaken for a service or installation limit? |
| Project-specific evidence | Relevant fire, wind, root or chemical requirements | Does the evidence address the actual assembly or exposure? |

Not every project needs the same tests. Equally, a long list of standards is not a substitute for identifying which requirement each document satisfies. Confirm the applicable standard and edition without treating different national classifications as automatic equivalents.

## Distinguish a requirement, a typical value and a test result

**A minimum requirement is an acceptance limit; a typical value describes the supplier's stated reference performance; a test result records a particular test.** They are not interchangeable simply because they share the same units.

Ask whether the quotation contractually commits to the required limit, which evidence supports it, and how conformity will be checked. A typical value above the requirement does not, by itself, guarantee that every roll meets the acceptance limit. Agree the test or inspection basis rather than assuming every property will be tested on every roll.

## Keep sheet properties separate from roof-system evidence

For wind or fire requirements, identify the roof assembly to which the evidence applies: deck, insulation, cover board where used, attachment and membrane. Changing one of these can change whether the cited evidence is relevant. A strong sheet tensile result alone does not establish wind-uplift resistance for the installed roof.

For industrial exposure, name the substances, concentration and exposure conditions to be assessed. Do not treat “PVC” or “chemical resistant” as approval for every chemical. Green roofs likewise need evidence for the proposed application, rather than a root-resistance assumption based on the material name.

Use the [roof waterproofing application page](/applications/roof-waterproofing/) to frame the build-up discussion. This article is a document-review checklist, not a fastening layout or welding procedure. Installation settings and acceptance checks must come from the applicable instructions and site verification.

## Close the gaps before confirming an order

Put unresolved items into the quotation discussion: missing document revisions, unclear thickness definitions, unidentified accessories or evidence that covers another configuration. Ask for written clarification and record any departures from the project specification.

Then connect the accepted specification to the delivery. Agree the required roll labels, product code, batch identification, dimensions and documents to accompany the goods. Separate the nominal area on a roll from the net coverage after the specified laps, cutting and detailing allowances. Compare price on the same supply and delivery basis.

### What should importers and project buyers confirm before a bulk order?

- **Importers and distributors:** confirm the stock configuration, repeat-order identification, minimum order quantity, sample availability, label requirements and packing. Ask whether private-label requirements can be met; do not assume they are included.
- **Project procurement teams:** identify the specified assembly, approval process, required documents, accessories and installation responsibilities. Flag proposed substitutions before the order is accepted.
- **Both:** record quotation currency, unit basis, quantity, destination, delivery terms, lead time, validity and exclusions. Compare like-for-like supply rather than roll price alone.

These are enquiry fields, not a statement of Tianyuan's current MOQ, stock, certification or delivery commitment.
