## SBS Basement Waterproofing: Choose by Access and Construction Sequence

**SBS membrane is an option for basement walls, slabs and foundations when its installation method matches the approved below-grade design.** Start with access to the water-facing side, the slab construction sequence and the joint layout. Then select the product, layer count, bonding method and protection. A thicker roll or a different reinforcement carrier cannot compensate for an inaccessible installation face or an incomplete joint detail.

This guide focuses on the site decisions and inspection records needed before waterproofing is covered. For a system overview, see [Underground Waterproofing Solutions](/applications/underground-waterproofing/). The [SBS membrane product page](/products/sbs-bitumen-waterproofing-membrane-manufacturer/) provides the available product configurations.

## Match Each Basement Zone to Its Installation Sequence

| Zone | Decision before selecting material | What to show on the drawing |
|---|---|---|
| Accessible external basement wall | Can the cured wall be prepared and waterproofed before backfill? | Bonding method, wall-to-slab connection, top termination and protection |
| Blind-side wall against shoring | Will the waterproofing face become inaccessible when the wall is cast? | A system approved for that sequence, concrete-facing orientation and connection to the base slab |
| Below the foundation slab | Is the membrane bonded to blinding, or must it bond to the later structural concrete? | Blinding preparation, membrane, any protection layer and continuity through the wall junction |
| Construction or movement joint | What movement and water pressure must the joint accommodate? | Compatible waterstop or joint system, membrane transition and inspection access |
| Buried podium or planted slab | Is the membrane exposed to roots, drainage layers or later landscaping works? | Specified root protection, drainage, terminations and protection from subsequent trades |

A torch-applied sheet bonded to blinding before a raft pour is not the same as a pre-applied membrane that bonds to fresh structural concrete. Use the [pre-applied HDPE membrane range](/products/pre-applied-hdpe-membrane-supplier/) when considering the latter, and obtain approval of the complete system.

## Torch-Applied SBS, Self-Adhesive Bitumen or Pre-Applied Membrane?

**Torch-applied SBS:** assess it where the substrate and access permit the specified bonding method and site controls allow hot work. Confirm primer compatibility, substrate condition, lap treatment and protection before the next trade.

**Self-adhesive bitumen:** assess it where a suitable torch-free bonding system is needed. It still requires the specified surface preparation, application conditions and lap pressure. It is not automatically suitable for a wet wall, standing water or every confined-space condition. See the [self-adhesive membrane configurations](/products/self-adhesive-bitumen-waterproofing-membrane-wholesale/).

**Pre-applied concrete-bonding membrane:** assess it where the design requires waterproofing installed before the concrete and a bond to that later pour. Do not infer bonding performance from the word HDPE alone. Product construction, seam details and the installation method matter.

A product designed for one sequence should not be reassigned to another without a documented system detail. [Sika's below-grade system overview](https://www.sika.com/en/construction/waterproofing-systems/below-grade-structures-basements.html) illustrates the use of different membrane and joint systems; its product approvals do not apply to other manufacturers.

## Specify Grade and Reinforcement Without Overstating Performance

Request a TDS for the actual reinforcement, thickness, surfacing and grade being supplied. Compare low-temperature flexibility, tensile and elongation results, watertightness test conditions, seam properties and the intended use. Values for one configuration should not be presented as the performance of every SBS roll.

- **Thickness and layer count:** follow the design and selected system documentation. Do not choose a universal single-layer build for all basement depths.
- **Reinforcement:** compare the required mechanical behaviour and dimensional stability. A higher tensile result is not a direct measurement of puncture resistance.
- **Movement joints:** PY, G or PYG describes a carrier, not a complete joint solution. Use an engineered joint assembly for the specified displacement and groundwater conditions.
- **Watertightness:** a short laboratory pressure test does not independently establish allowable long-term groundwater head.
- **Surfacing and bonding:** verify compatibility with the primer, adjacent membrane, protection layer and the chosen application method.

For more detail, use the [SBS grade comparison](/blog/sbs-membrane-type-i-vs-type-ii-guide/), [reinforcement comparison](/blog/py-g-pyg-sbs-membrane-reinforcement-guide/) and [SBS TDS reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-sbs-membrane-tds/). Confirm the applicable test standard and edition on the supplied report rather than relying on a generic online table.

## Wall-to-Slab Connections and Penetrations Need Their Own Details

Mark every change of plane and material before ordering. At the base of a wall, confirm how the slab membrane remains accessible for connection and protected during wall construction. At pipe entries, show the sleeve, compatible sealing arrangement and membrane termination. At a movement joint, show the joint system and its transition into the field membrane.

Avoid assuming that a strip of reinforced sheet bridges every joint movement. Have the designer review difficult transitions before they are covered. If below-grade works connect to a tunnel or shaft, coordinate the change of system using the [tunnel waterproofing selection guide](/blog/tunnel-waterproofing-membrane-selection/).

## Site Inspection Checklist Before Covering

| Stage | Check and record | Hold work when |
|---|---|---|
| Substrate acceptance | Surface preparation, repairs and moisture conditions required by the product | Loose material, standing water or unapproved surface defects remain |
| Primer and bonding | Compatible products and application conditions; coverage and cure as specified | Primer is contaminated or bonding conditions are outside the method statement |
| Membrane placement | Correct orientation, adhesion and lap widths from the approved method | Wrinkles, open laps, trapped debris or unbonded areas remain |
| Corners and penetrations | Detail drawings, compatible components and completed terminations | A transition has no agreed detail or cannot be inspected |
| Testing | The project-specified inspection or integrity test, with recorded acceptance criteria | A defect or incomplete test remains unresolved |
| Protection and backfill | Compatible protection, suitable backfill and control of following trades | Protection is displaced or the membrane is exposed to construction damage |

Use the approved test method for the system and the accessible area. Flood testing, seam testing and electrical integrity testing are not interchangeable. Repair and reinspect defects before concealing them. For torch-applied sheet defects, see the [SBS bubbles and repair guide](/blog/bubbles-in-sbs-membrane-torch-application-guide/).

## Protection and Drainage Are Separate Decisions

Protection prevents damage during later works; drainage manages water under the designed ground-water strategy. Specify each function rather than assuming one board performs both. Check compatibility with the membrane and how protection is supported at corners and the wall toe. A [geotextile filter or separation layer](/products/geotextile-fabric-manufacturer/) may be part of the detail, but it does not replace the structural design, membrane or an engineered drainage arrangement.

Retain photographs and inspection releases by wall or slab zone before backfill. When another trade damages the waterproofing, record the repair location and the reinspection result rather than covering it on a verbal instruction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can SBS membrane be used for basement waterproofing?

SBS membrane can form part of an approved basement waterproofing system on suitable substrates. Select the product, application method, joints and protection for the construction sequence and project design.

### Can SBS membrane be installed below a foundation slab?

A membrane may be installed over a prepared blinding layer before the structural slab where the specified system permits it. This does not mean it bonds to the later concrete pour; use a dedicated pre-applied system when that bond is required.

### Does PYG reinforcement replace movement-joint waterproofing?

No. A membrane carrier is not a movement-joint assembly. Joint movement, water pressure and transitions require an engineered detail with compatible components.

### What thickness should I use for basement waterproofing?

Use the thickness, layer count and reinforcement required by the approved project design and the selected product documentation. Thickness alone does not establish suitability for groundwater pressure.

### Do I need protection before backfilling?

Yes. Specify compatible membrane protection and suitable backfill, inspect the waterproofing before covering, and repair damage before proceeding.
