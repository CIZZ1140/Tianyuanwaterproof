quick_facts:
  - title: "Open the Drum. That's the Entire Mixing Procedure."
    text: "Two-component polyurethane demands on-site ratio measurement — resin and curing agent, weighed or estimated, mixed, and poured before the pot life expires. In a 35 °C Southeast Asian afternoon, the mixed batch has roughly two hours before it solidifies in the bucket. Single-component PU eliminates all of this. The drum contains finished product. Pour what you need. Seal the rest. No measured mixing means no mixing errors — and the waste from expired mixed material drops to zero. For a project manager running multiple trades on a tight schedule, that zero is worth more than any per-kilogram material saving."

  - title: "It Cures Faster When the Air Is Humid — That's Reversed Logic, and It Works"
    text: "Most construction materials hate moisture. PU doesn't just tolerate it — it consumes it. The isocyanate groups in the prepolymer react with atmospheric water to form urea linkages, crosslinking into a three-dimensional elastic network. At 70% relative humidity — typical for coastal Southeast Asia and South China — the reaction accelerates. The substrate doesn't need to be bone-dry; damp concrete with no standing water is an acceptable surface. Where [SBS modified bitumen membranes](/products/sbs-bitumen-waterproofing-membrane-manufacturer/) require a dry primer-coated substrate, PU turns ambient moisture from a construction delay into a curing accelerant."

  - title: "It Goes Where Sheet Membranes Physically Cannot"
    text: "Three pipe penetrations intersecting in a corner. An elevator pit with a drainage channel running diagonally across the floor. The inside curve of a domed roof. These geometries are why sheet membrane installers spend half their time cutting, lapping, and sealing pieces together — each seam is a potential entry point for water. PU is a liquid. Pour it, spread it, and it self-levels into the lowest point. No cut pieces. No overlap seams. No torch. The cured film is one continuous molecule across the entire surface — a seamless, joint-free elastomeric waterproofing membrane."

  - title: "The Concrete Breaks Before the Bond Does"
    text: "Pull-off adhesion testing on PU-coated concrete routinely produces 100% cohesive failure: the fracture occurs inside the concrete substrate, not at the PU-concrete interface. The measured bond strength (≥0.50 MPa on damp substrate, higher on dry) exceeds the tensile capacity of the concrete surface layer. This is not a marketing claim — it's the standard failure mode required by GB/T 19250 for qualified product. For an underground basement wall under hydrostatic pressure, where groundwater is physically trying to push the waterproofing layer off the concrete, cohesive failure mode means the bond cannot be peeled away by the pressure it's resisting."

application_guide:
  - title: "Substrate Preparation — Flat, Sound, No Standing Water"
    text: "Remove loose material, dust, and oil. Grind down high spots. Fill cracks wider than 2 mm with repair mortar. The substrate can be damp — dark in color, cool to the touch — but there must be no standing water. For basements, pump out any pooled water and allow 24 hours of ventilation before application. No primer is required on sound concrete."
  - title: "Detail Treatment — Corners and Penetrations First, Not After"
    text: "Apply 2–4 extra coats at all inside and outside corners, pipe penetrations, and floor drains before the main area. Build thickness to ≥1 mm at these points. If reinforcement fabric is specified, embed polyester non-woven fabric into the wet coating at this stage — press firmly with a scraper to fully saturate the fabric and eliminate air pockets."
  - title: "Main Area Application — Cross-Coating in Two Directions"
    text: "Apply the first coat in one direction using a rubber squeegee or short-nap roller. Wait until tack-free — approximately 4–6 hours at 25°C, longer below 15°C. Apply the second coat perpendicular to the first. The cross-direction pattern seals pinholes that may have formed in the first coat. Each coat consumes approximately 0.8–1.0 kg/m². Total film thickness depends on the application: 1.2 mm for bathrooms, 1.5 mm for roofs, 2 mm minimum for pools and basements."
  - title: "Reinforcement — Between Coat One and Coat Two"
    text: "For crack-repair zones, cold joints, and new-to-old concrete interfaces, lay glass-fiber mesh or polyester non-woven fabric into the first coat while it is still wet. Press from the center outward with a scraper to expel all trapped air. The fabric must be fully saturated — no dry fibers visible on the surface. Overlap adjacent fabric sections by at least 100 mm."
  - title: "Sand Broadcasting — Mechanical Key for Tile Adhesive"
    text: "If the PU surface will receive a cementitious screed or tile adhesive, broadcast clean, dry medium-coarse sand into the final wet coat. The sand particles embed in the curing PU and provide mechanical interlock. No separate bonding primer is required — the tile adhesive bonds directly to the sand-roughened surface."
  - title: "Curing Protection — Keep It Dry Until Set"
    text: "Do not allow foot traffic on the wet coating. At 25°C, the surface is walkable after approximately 24 hours. In cooler conditions — below 10°C — allow 48 hours. Protect fresh coating from rain: water droplets on uncured PU cause surface blistering. Once fully cured, the coating is resistant to temporary water exposure."

faqs:
  - question: "How is single-component PU applied, and how does it cure?"
    answer: "The coating is supplied as a ready-to-use liquid in a sealed drum. Apply with a rubber squeegee or short-nap roller at 0.8–1.0 kg/m² per coat. Curing occurs through a chemical reaction between isocyanate groups in the prepolymer and atmospheric moisture — no catalyst, no heating, and no on-site mixing are involved. Tack-free time is ≤12 hours at 23°C. Full hardness develops within 24 hours. Below 5°C, the moisture-cure reaction slows significantly — postpone application or use forced-air heating set below 60°C."

  - question: "Type I vs Type II — which should I specify?"
    answer: "Type I (≥550% elongation, ≥1.9 MPa tensile): for substrates that move — roofs under thermal cycling, expansion joints, seismic-zone buildings, and concrete slabs expected to develop shrinkage cracks. The high elongation absorbs substrate movement without tearing. Type II (≥2.45 MPa tensile, ≥450% elongation): for structures under sustained hydrostatic pressure — basements, tunnels, swimming pools, water tanks, and below-grade walls. The higher tensile strength resists the force of groundwater trying to push through the membrane. Both types pass -40°C low-temperature bend and 0.3 MPa watertightness testing."

  - question: "Can PU be applied on a damp concrete surface?"
    answer: "Yes — damp is acceptable. Standing water is not. A concrete surface that is dark from moisture but has no visible water film can be coated directly. PU's moisture-cure mechanism actively consumes ambient humidity, so the residual moisture in green concrete does not inhibit curing — it participates in it. For freshly stripped basement walls, pump out any pooled water, ventilate for 24 hours, and proceed. The GB/T 19250 damp-base adhesion requirement of ≥0.50 MPa confirms performance on substrates that have not fully dried."

  - question: "How long does it take to dry under different temperatures?"
    answer: "At 25°C: tack-free in 4–6 hours, fully cured in 24 hours. At 15°C: tack-free in 8–12 hours, fully cured in 36 hours. Below 10°C: tack-free may exceed 12 hours, full cure may take 48 hours or longer. The practical test: press a dry finger onto the coating — if it leaves no fingerprint and feels dry to the touch, the next coat can be applied. For accelerated curing in cold conditions, use industrial fan heaters with the air temperature kept below 60°C to avoid surface skinning that traps uncured material underneath."

  - question: "How many coats and what is the coverage rate?"
    answer: "Minimum two coats applied in perpendicular directions — the cross-coating pattern seals pinholes that may form in a single-direction application. Each coat consumes 0.8–1.0 kg/m². Total film thickness targets: 1.2 mm for interior wet areas (bathrooms, kitchens), 1.5 mm for protected roofs, 2 mm minimum for swimming pools and underground structures. Achieving a 1 mm dry film thickness requires approximately 1.6–1.8 kg/m² of wet material. Internal and external corners and pipe penetrations receive 2–4 extra coats before the main area application — building to ≥1 mm before the first full coat."

  - question: "Can it be used on exposed roofs? Will sunlight degrade it?"
    answer: "Yes — with the correct formulation. The standard product is aromatic PU, designed for buried, internal, and protected applications. For exposed roofs, gutters, and balconies subject to direct UV radiation, specify the aliphatic PU variant. Aliphatic PU passes GB/T 19250 artificial weathering testing (no cracking or deformation after accelerated UV exposure) and is referenced by ASTM D7311 for single-pack moisture-triggered aliphatic roofing membranes. Specify 'exposed roof application' at order stage so the correct formula is dispatched. For exposed roofs, do not reduce the coating thickness — UV penetrates from the surface downward; at least 2 mm dry film thickness is recommended."

### Why PU Handles Complex Geometry Better Than Sheet Goods

A basement slab with three pipe penetrations converging at one corner, a drainage channel running diagonally, and an elevator pit with compound-angle inside corners. A 1-meter-wide [SBS modified bitumen membrane](/products/sbs-bitumen-waterproofing-membrane-manufacturer/) installer cuts seven or eight separate pieces to fit this single detail — each lap seam is a potential leak path.

PU does not cut and fit. It is a liquid. Pour it in, spread it, and it self-levels into the lowest point, filling annular spaces around pipes and rounding inside corners naturally. The cured film is one continuous, seamless elastomeric membrane — chemically formed on the substrate, not mechanically assembled from factory-cut pieces. Installation time at complex nodes is typically one-third or less of the equivalent sheet membrane detailing.
